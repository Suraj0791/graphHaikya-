# So can I promise "nothing will ever be left"?

I won't say **100%**, because that wouldn't be honest.

Algorithms like **Maximum Flow**, **Heavy-Light Decomposition**, or **Blossom** exist, and they are part of graph algorithms. If one day you target **ICPC World Finals**, **IOI**, or very advanced competitive programming, you'll eventually learn those too.

**But I can confidently say this:**

If we complete everything through **Part 14** the way we've planned—understanding first principles, not memorizing—you'll have mastered the graph knowledge needed for **virtually every LeetCode graph problem, the vast majority of OA questions, and almost all graph interviews for software engineering roles.**

And here's the promise I'm most comfortable making:

> **After that roadmap, if I give you a brand-new graph problem you've never seen before, your first reaction won't be "Which algorithm is this?" It will be "How do I model it? What's the objective? Which engine? Which plugins?"**

To me, **that** is what "mastering graphs" actually means. It's not knowing every named algorithm in existence—it's having a mental operating system that lets you derive the right approach for almost every interview problem you encounter.
